Stop Paying Adobe: Affinity Photo is Now Free

For decades, Adobe Photoshop has been the undisputed king of image editing. But in late 2025, the landscape shifted dramatically when Canva acquired Serif and announced that Affinity Photo—arguably the most capable Photoshop alternative ever created—would become completely free. This is not a trial, a limited version, or a student discount. The full professional application, with every feature, every tool, and every workspace, now costs exactly zero dollars.

If you are currently paying $9.99, $22.99, or $54.99 monthly for Adobe Creative Cloud, this development represents a potential savings of $120–$660 per year. The video guide above explores the practical reality of replacing Adobe with Affinity, testing whether this newly free software can truly handle professional photography workflows. Here is everything you need to know about making the switch.

1. The News: Affinity Photo is Now Free

In November 2025, Canva (which acquired Serif, the makers of Affinity) announced that the entire Affinity suite would be rebranded as Affinity Studio and made free for everyone. This includes what was formerly Affinity Photo (now called the "Pixel" workspace), Affinity Designer (now "Vector"), and Affinity Publisher (now "Layout"). All three professional applications are now housed within a single, unified interface that costs nothing to download and use.

What is free: Every core feature from the previous paid versions. RAW development, layer-based editing, advanced masking, vector tools, layout and publishing capabilities, export options, and file format support. The free version receives regular updates and new features at no cost.

What requires Canva Pro: Canva's integrated AI tools within the Affinity interface. If you want AI-powered features like generative fill, background removal, or smart resize, you will need a Canva Pro subscription. But for traditional, professional editing—the kind photographers have done for decades—everything is completely free.

2. The Financial Reality: Calculating Your Savings

Adobe's subscription model has generated significant frustration among creatives who prefer to own their tools rather than rent them indefinitely. Let us examine the real costs over time:

  • Photography Plan ($9.99/month): $119.88 per year, $599.40 over 5 years, $1,198.80 over 10 years
  • Single App ($22.99/month): $275.88 per year, $1,379.40 over 5 years, $2,758.80 over 10 years
  • All Apps ($54.99/month): $659.88 per year, $3,299.40 over 5 years, $6,598.80 over 10 years

Affinity Studio, by contrast, costs $0. The money you save can fund new camera equipment, printing supplies, travel for photography projects, or simply remain in your bank account. For hobbyists, students, and emerging professionals, the removal of this financial barrier is transformative.

3. Feature Comparison: Can Affinity Replace Photoshop?

The critical question: does the free Affinity Studio provide the tools necessary for professional photography work? The answer is overwhelmingly yes for most users, with some notable caveats for specialized workflows.

Where Affinity Matches or Exceeds Photoshop:

  • RAW Processing: The Develop Persona (formerly Affinity Photo's Camera Raw equivalent) provides comprehensive RAW adjustments including exposure, contrast, curves, HSL, lens corrections, and detailed sharpening. It handles all major RAW formats including CR3, NEF, ARW, and ORF.
  • Layer-Based Editing: Full non-destructive editing with adjustment layers, masks, blend modes, and groups. Layers behave like Smart Objects by default—you can resize repeatedly without quality loss.
  • Selection Tools: Object Selection automatically detects subjects with edge-aware precision comparable to Photoshop's AI selection. The Selection Brush, Flood Select (Magic Wand equivalent), and Refine Edge tools handle complex cutouts including hair and fur.
  • Retouching: Clone Stamp, Healing Brush, Patch tools, Frequency Separation for skin retouching, and Dodge & Burn tools all function identically to Photoshop.
  • Color Correction: Curves (with RGB/CMYK/LAB modes), Levels, White Balance, Color Balance, and advanced color grading tools. The Haze Removal filter actually outperforms Photoshop's equivalent, offering distance-based control that preserves atmospheric perspective while clearing foreground haze.
  • Filters and Effects: Extensive filter gallery including Gaussian Blur, Lens Blur, Motion Blur, Sharpen, Unsharp Mask, Distort, Noise reduction, Lighting effects, and Shadows/Highlights. All filters are non-destructive "Live Filters" that remain editable.
  • Liquify: Dedicated Liquify Persona with mesh-based warping, reconstruction, and freeze mask capabilities identical to Photoshop's Liquify filter.
  • HDR and Tone Mapping: Dedicated Tone Mapping Persona with presets (Natural, Detailed, Cool, High Contrast Black & White, Dramatic) and full manual control over tone compression and local contrast.
  • File Compatibility: Opens layered PSD files preserving masks, groups, and adjustments. Exports to PSD (with some limitations), TIFF, JPEG, PNG, and PDF.

4. The Affinity Interface: Familiar Yet Improved

Affinity Studio deliberately mirrors Photoshop's layout to minimize the learning curve. The toolbar runs down the left side. Tool properties display across the top context bar. Layers, adjustments, and histograms live in the right panel. The keyboard shortcuts are largely identical—Ctrl/Cmd+S for save, Ctrl/Cmd+Z for undo, B for brush, V for move, L for lasso.

However, Affinity introduces several interface improvements that many users prefer:

  • Personas: Different workspaces for different tasks. Photo (standard editing), Liquify (warping), Develop (RAW processing), Tone Mapping (HDR), and Export (batch processing). Each persona presents only the relevant tools, reducing interface clutter.
  • Separated Mode: Float all panels and documents independently across multiple monitors. More flexible than Photoshop's docked interface.
  • Live Filter Layers: All filters apply as editable layers rather than destructive permanent changes. Return and adjust settings at any point in your workflow.
  • Adjustment Thumbnails: Each adjustment layer displays a thumbnail preview of its effect, making it easy to identify which layer controls what.
  • Transform Panel: Shows exact dimensions and rotation angles while scaling, eliminating guesswork.
  • Infinite Undo: No history panel limitations—go back to your first action if needed.

5. Where Photoshop Still Leads

Despite Affinity's impressive capabilities, Adobe maintains advantages in specific areas:

  • AI-Powered Features: Generative Fill, Generative Expand, Content-Aware Fill's most advanced iterations, and Neural Filters remain Adobe exclusives. These AI tools can save significant time on complex compositing tasks.
  • Industry Standard: Photoshop is the universal language of creative professionals. Collaboration, file sharing, and plugin ecosystems remain Adobe-centric. Many professional workflows assume PSD compatibility.
  • Plugin Ecosystem: While Affinity supports many Photoshop plugins, the full range of specialized tools (Topaz, Nik Collection's deepest integrations, certain retouching panels) works best within Photoshop.
  • Cloud Integration: Adobe Creative Cloud offers seamless synchronization across devices, shared libraries, and cloud document storage. Affinity is standalone software requiring manual file management.
  • Video and 3D: Photoshop includes robust video editing layers and 3D modeling capabilities that Affinity does not attempt to match.
  • Smart Objects: Photoshop's Smart Object system, with embedded linked files and non-destructive scaling, remains more sophisticated than Affinity's layer behavior.

For photographers who rely heavily on AI generative features, work in large creative teams using Adobe's collaboration tools, or need video editing integration, Photoshop retains clear advantages worth the subscription cost.

6. The Migration Process: Switching Workflows

Transitioning from Photoshop to Affinity requires adjustment, but the process is smoother than most software migrations:

File Migration: Your existing PSD files open in Affinity with layers, masks, and groups intact. Smart Objects may rasterize, and some adjustment layer types might flatten, but core editability is preserved. Going forward, save new work in Affinity's native format (.afphoto) for maximum compatibility.

Learning Curve: Expect 2–4 weeks of adjustment. While interfaces are similar, terminology differs: "Content-Aware Fill" becomes "Inpainting," "Camera Raw Filter" becomes "Develop Persona," "Smart Filters" become "Live Filters." The video tutorial above demonstrates these equivalents in practice.

Customization: Affinity allows extensive interface customization. Move panels, create custom toolbars, and save workspace layouts that match your Photoshop muscle memory. The Studio panel system lets you dock, float, or hide any panel combination.

Performance: Many users report Affinity loads faster and operates more responsively than Photoshop on equivalent hardware, particularly when working with large files or many layers.

7. Who Should Switch (and Who Shouldn't)

Affinity Studio is ideal for:

  • Hobbyists and Students: Remove the financial barrier to professional-grade editing tools. Learn on industry-standard workflows without subscription anxiety.
  • Independent Photographers: Solo practitioners who own their workflows and do not require extensive collaboration save significant money without sacrificing capability.
  • Print-Focused Professionals: Affinity's CMYK handling, soft proofing, and PDF export capabilities satisfy most print production needs.
  • Adobe Frustrated Users: Those tired of subscription costs, mandatory cloud connections, or Adobe's increasingly aggressive monetization.
  • Multi-Platform Users: Affinity works identically across Windows, macOS, and iPad without requiring cloud synchronization or platform-specific subscriptions.

Photoshop remains necessary for:

  • Large Creative Teams: Organizations standardized on Adobe Creative Cloud for collaboration, asset libraries, and shared workflows.
  • AI-Dependent Workflows: Photographers using Generative Fill, Neural Filters, or AI-powered selection as core productivity tools.
  • Motion Graphics Professionals: Those requiring video editing, animation, or 3D integration within their image editor.
  • Plugin-Dependent Users: Workflows built around specific Photoshop-exclusive plugins or panels.
  • Industry Veterans: Those with 20+ years of Photoshop muscle memory who prioritize familiarity over cost savings.

8. The Bigger Picture: Canva's Strategy

Understanding why Affinity is now free helps predict its future. Canva acquired Serif to compete with Adobe in the professional creative space. By making Affinity Studio free, Canva aims to:

  • Capture the next generation of creatives who start with free tools
  • Upsell Canva Pro subscriptions for AI features to power users
  • Build an ecosystem that transitions users from Canva's simple design tools to Affinity's professional capabilities
  • Challenge Adobe's monopoly through market disruption

This strategy suggests Affinity's core features will remain free indefinitely—Canva benefits from widespread adoption even without direct payment. The company has committed to ongoing development and free updates, distinguishing this from abandoned freeware.

9. Getting Started: Download and Setup

Transitioning to Affinity Studio takes minutes:

  1. Visit affinity.studio and create a free Canva account (required for download authentication)
  2. Download Affinity Studio for your operating system (Windows, macOS, or iPad)
  3. Install and sign in with your Canva credentials
  4. Choose your workspace: Pixel (photo editing), Vector (illustration), or Layout (publishing)
  5. Open a test PSD file to verify layer compatibility with your existing work
  6. Customize the interface via View > Studio to match your preferred layout

The application does not require ongoing internet connectivity (except for initial authentication and updates), works offline indefinitely, and imposes no usage restrictions or watermarks.

10. The Verdict: A New Era for Creative Software

Affinity Studio's free release represents the most significant disruption in creative software since Adobe introduced Creative Cloud subscriptions in 2012. For the first time, professional-grade image editing, vector design, and publishing tools are available to everyone without financial barrier.

For photographers specifically, the Pixel workspace provides everything needed for RAW processing, retouching, compositing, and print preparation. The money saved on Adobe subscriptions can fund better lenses, printing equipment, or photography education. The learning curve is manageable, the interface is polished, and the capabilities are genuinely professional.

Adobe Photoshop remains the industry standard and offers unmatched AI features, but it is no longer the only path to professional results. For thousands of photographers, the question has shifted from "Can I afford to edit professionally?" to "Why am I still paying rent for software I could own for free?"
