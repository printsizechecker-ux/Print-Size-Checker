Can You Print AI-Generated Images? A Practical Guide for Artists and Photographers

Written by Marcus Webb Published July 21, 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: Yes, you can print AI-generated images — but there's a resolution limit that most people hit before they expect to. This guide explains exactly what print sizes are achievable from Midjourney, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, and other AI tools, and what to do when your image isn't big enough to print at the size you want.

Why AI Images and Printing Don't Always Get Along

AI image generators like Midjourney, DALL-E 3, and Stable Diffusion produce visually stunning results. The challenge is that they generate images at a fixed pixel resolution — and that resolution determines how large the image can be printed without looking soft or pixelated.

Most people don't realise this until they've already ordered a print. The image looks flawless on screen, but on a 12×16 inch canvas it looks blurry. The screen was hiding the problem — monitors display images at 72–96 pixels per inch, which flatters low-resolution files. Printers expose every pixel.

The good news is this is entirely predictable. If you know what resolution your AI tool produces, you can calculate exactly what print sizes will work — before ordering anything.

What Resolution Do AI Image Generators Actually Produce?

Every AI image generator has a default output resolution. Here's what the major tools produce out of the box:

AI Tool Default Output Max Without External Upscaling Aspect Ratios Available
Midjourney v6 1024 × 1024 px 4096 × 4096 px (4x upscale) Square, portrait, landscape, custom
DALL-E 3 1024 × 1024 px 1792 × 1024 px (wide) / 1024 × 1792 px (tall) Square, wide, tall
Stable Diffusion XL 1024 × 1024 px 2048 × 2048 px (with Hi-Res Fix) Many ratios via prompting
Adobe Firefly 1024 × 1024 px 2048 × 2048 px Square, landscape, portrait, widescreen
Flux (Black Forest) 1024 × 1024 px 2048 × 2048 px (dev model) Square, custom via parameters
Stable Diffusion 1.5 512 × 512 px 768 × 768 px base, more with upscalers Square, custom

The pattern is clear: most modern AI tools produce a base image of 1024 × 1024 pixels. Some allow built-in upscaling to 2048 or 4096 pixels. Older models like SD 1.5 start at just 512 pixels, which severely limits print options without external upscaling.

What Print Sizes Can You Actually Achieve?

This is where resolution meets reality. To calculate the maximum print size at a given quality level, divide the image's pixel dimensions by the DPI (dots per inch) you want to print at.

The formula: Print size (inches) = Pixel dimension ÷ DPI

For example: a 1024 pixel dimension ÷ 300 DPI = 3.41 inches. That same image at 150 DPI = 6.83 inches.

Higher resolution AI outputs unlock larger print sizes. Most default AI images (1024px) max out around 6×8 inches at acceptable quality.

Here's the same data as a quick reference table:

AI Output Resolution Max Size @ 300 DPI Max Size @ 150 DPI Good for
512 × 512 px (SD 1.5) 1.7 × 1.7 in 3.4 × 3.4 in Screen only — not suitable for printing
1024 × 1024 px (most AI tools) 3.4 × 3.4 in 6.8 × 6.8 in Small prints, greeting cards, social posts
1024 × 1792 px (DALL-E portrait) 3.4 × 6 in 6.8 × 12 in Small portrait prints
2048 × 2048 px (Midjourney 2x) 6.8 × 6.8 in 13.6 × 13.6 in Medium prints, social media art
4096 × 4096 px (Midjourney 4x) 13.6 × 13.6 in 27.3 × 27.3 in Large wall art, posters, canvas

Important: These are maximum sizes at those DPI levels. Viewing distance matters too — a 24×36 inch poster viewed from six feet away doesn't need 300 DPI to look sharp. If your image falls slightly short of the numbers above, check our guide to DPI and viewing distance before giving up on the print size you want.

The Resolution Problem Nobody Talks About

There's a subtlety that catches even experienced AI artists off guard. When you generate a non-square image — say, a landscape at 2:1 aspect ratio — the longer dimension might be 2048 pixels, but the shorter dimension is only 1024 pixels. The print size is constrained by the shorter dimension.

For example, Midjourney at --ar 2:1 might produce an image at 2048 × 1024 pixels. At 300 DPI, that's 6.8 × 3.4 inches — not the large landscape print you imagined. The 2048 pixel wide dimension is impressive, but the 1024 pixel height limits what you can actually produce.

This is why checking your actual pixel dimensions (not just the megapixel count) before ordering matters. A 2MP image that's 2000 × 1000 pixels prints very differently to a 2MP image that's 1414 × 1414 pixels.

How to Make AI Images Large Enough to Print

If your AI image is too low resolution for the print size you want, you have three options:

1. Use Your AI Tool's Built-in Upscaler

Most AI image generators include an upscaling option. Midjourney's "U" upscale buttons and "upscale (subtle)" / "upscale (creative)" options can take a 1024px image to 2048px or 4096px with reasonable quality. This is always the first step — use the tool that created the image to enlarge it, because it understands the original content best.

2. Use a Dedicated AI Upscaling Tool

For images that need to go beyond what the source tool offers, dedicated AI upscalers deliver significantly better results than traditional upscaling. Topaz Gigapixel AI and Magnific AI are the two most respected options for print-quality upscaling. Both can typically take a 1024×1024 image to 4096×4096 or larger while maintaining sharp detail — putting large format printing firmly within reach.

For a deeper look at how AI upscaling works for print, see our guide: AI Upscaling: Print Small Files Large.

3. Regenerate at a Higher Resolution

If you're still working in the AI tool and haven't finalised your image, use higher resolution settings from the start. Midjourney's --q 2 flag and high-variation upscale modes produce sharper source images. With SDXL, running a high-res fix pass before exporting improves the base resolution significantly.

The Right Workflow: Check Before You Print

The most common (and most avoidable) mistake is ordering a print without first verifying the resolution is adequate for the size. Here's the workflow that prevents wasted print orders:

  1. Generate your AI image and apply any built-in upscaling available.
  2. Check the pixel dimensions of your exported file (right-click → Properties → Details on Windows, or Get Info on Mac).
  3. Use PrintSizeChecker.com to upload your image and see instantly whether it has enough resolution for your target print size — and at what DPI.
  4. If the quality rating is GOOD or EXCELLENT at your target size, order. If it's POOR or FAIR, upscale first.
  5. Export for print in TIFF or PNG format (avoid JPEG for final print files — compression artifacts become visible at large sizes).

What About Copyright? Can AI Images Be Printed and Sold?

This is a separate question from resolution, but a common one. The legal position on AI-generated image copyright is still evolving in most jurisdictions, and it varies by country and by which AI tool you used. Generally:

  • Images generated entirely by AI (with no human creative input beyond a text prompt) may not qualify for copyright protection in some countries, including the US under current guidance.
  • Midjourney's terms of service allow commercial use for paid subscribers.
  • DALL-E 3 (via OpenAI) grants you usage rights for commercial purposes.
  • Stable Diffusion outputs are generally considered open for commercial use depending on the model's licence.

The resolution question is simpler to answer — the legal question requires checking the specific terms of whichever tool you used and ideally consulting a legal professional for commercial applications.

Conclusion

Yes, you can absolutely print AI-generated images — and the results can be spectacular. The key is understanding that most AI tools produce images at 1024 pixels by default, which limits print size to about 3–7 inches at quality settings before upscaling is needed. Using your AI tool's built-in upscaler, or a dedicated tool like Topaz Gigapixel, gets you to print-ready sizes for almost any format.
