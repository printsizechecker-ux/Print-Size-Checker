About PrintSizeChecker

A free tool built by a photographer who got tired of wasted print orders — and decided to fix the problem properly.

Marcus Webb

Photographer, Developer & Founder of PrintSizeChecker

I've been photographing professionally for over a decade — weddings, landscapes, portraiture, and commercial work. In that time I've sent more files to print labs than I can count. And I've wasted more money than I'd like to admit on prints that came back blurry, pixelated, or just plain wrong because I didn't realise the image wasn't large enough for the size I'd ordered.

At some point I stopped accepting that as a normal part of the process and built a tool to fix it. That was PrintSizeChecker.

The Problem That Started All of This

The frustration that led to PrintSizeChecker is embarrassingly simple: printers and monitors don't speak the same language, and nobody tells you that until after you've wasted $60 on a canvas print that looks like it was run through a blender.

A photo can look absolutely stunning on a 27-inch retina display. Crisp, detailed, perfect. Then you order a 16×20 inch print, it arrives, and it's soft. Blurry around the edges. The colours are different. The detail you spent an hour editing is just... gone.

This happens because screens display images at 72–96 pixels per inch, which makes even relatively low-resolution images look sharp. Printers work at 300 dots per inch, which requires significantly more raw pixel data to produce the same level of sharpness. The screen was lying to you — and there was no easy way to know that before sending the file.

"I built PrintSizeChecker because I couldn't find a tool that gave me a straight answer: will this specific image look sharp at this specific print size? Not a formula, not a rule of thumb — an actual answer for my actual file."

I tried every online tool I could find. Most just converted pixels to inches using a fixed 300 DPI assumption and gave you a number. They didn't factor in that 200 DPI looks perfectly fine for a large canvas viewed from across the room. They didn't show you the visual difference between quality levels. They didn't warn you when something was borderline. They definitely didn't help you prepare the file for a specific print lab's requirements.

So I built something that did all of that — and kept it free, because the problem it solves shouldn't cost money to solve.

How the Tool Works

PrintSizeChecker takes your image, reads its pixel dimensions, and calculates exactly how large it can be printed at different quality levels. It doesn't guess, and it doesn't use fixed rules. It uses your actual file and your target print size to give you a specific quality rating.

Real-Time Quality Ratings

Instantly see whether your image is EXCELLENT, GOOD, FAIR, or POOR for your chosen print size — at 300 DPI, 200 DPI, and 150 DPI simultaneously.

100% Private

The images you check are processed in your browser and are never uploaded or stored — the photo itself never leaves your device. We do record limited anonymous usage stats (such as file dimensions and general location); see our Privacy Policy for details.

Smart Cropping Preview

See exactly how your image fits different print aspect ratios before you order — no surprises when the lab crops your photo to fit a standard frame size.

Print-Ready Download

Download your image pre-sized for your chosen print dimensions, cropped correctly, and ready to send to any professional lab.

The Story Behind the Site

The Problem

Wasted print orders, again

After one too many disappointing print deliveries — and a particularly frustrating 24×36 canvas that came back visibly pixelated — I decided to properly understand the maths behind print resolution. What I found surprised me: the tools available to photographers for checking this were genuinely terrible.

The Build

A weekend side project that kept growing

The first version of PrintSizeChecker took a weekend to build and did one thing: tell you if your image had enough pixels for a specific print size. It was ugly, it was basic, and photographers immediately found it useful. That was the signal to keep going.

Growing

Adding everything photographers actually asked for

Over time, the tool grew to include the crop preview, the add-white-border function, the print-ready download, and the full library of photography and printing guides. Every feature was added because real users asked for it, not because it seemed like a good idea in theory.

Today

Free and always will be

PrintSizeChecker has helped tens of thousands of photographers check print quality before ordering. It's still completely free, still doesn't require an account, and still doesn't upload your images anywhere. That's not going to change.

The Writing Behind the Guides

Every article on PrintSizeChecker is written by me — Marcus Webb — based on real experience with photography, printing, and the workflows that connect the two. I don't publish articles on topics I don't genuinely understand, and every guide is grounded in real photography and printing experience.

The guides cover topics I've dealt with personally: why monitors and printers show different colours, how to know if an iPhone photo will make a good print, what the actual difference between lustre and glossy paper is, which photo labs produce consistently good results, and how AI upscaling tools can recover a low-resolution file for large format printing.
