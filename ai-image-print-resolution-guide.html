AI Image Print Resolution Guide: Exact Specs for Midjourney, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion and More

Written by Marcus Webb Published July 22, 2026 · 9 min read

This guide provides the exact resolution specifications for every major AI image generator, and a complete reference table showing what print sizes are achievable from each tool — at both 300 DPI and 150 DPI. If you want to know whether a specific AI tool can produce a specific print size, you'll find the answer here.

Why AI Image Resolution Is Different to Camera Resolution

When photographers talk about image resolution, they're usually thinking about megapixels — the total number of pixels in an image. But with AI-generated images, there's a specific challenge that doesn't apply to camera photos: AI images are typically generated at a fixed square resolution, and that square constraint limits how large you can print in any format.

A 24-megapixel camera might produce a 6000 × 4000 pixel image — which prints beautifully at 20 × 13 inches at 300 DPI. But a 1-megapixel AI image at 1024 × 1024 pixels? That only prints at 3.4 × 3.4 inches at the same quality. The AI image has 24 times fewer pixels, and that difference is immediately visible in print.

Understanding exactly what your AI tool produces — and what it can produce with upscaling — is the starting point for getting AI art into print.

AI Tool Specifications: The Complete Breakdown

Here's what each major AI image generator produces, with the settings that matter for printing:

Midjourney Versions 5, 6, and 6.1

Midjourney is the most print-friendly of the major AI tools, primarily because it offers the most aggressive built-in upscaling options.

Default output
1024 × 1024 px
2x upscale (subtle/creative)
2048 × 2048 px
4x upscale (via "U" then upscale)
4096 × 4096 px
Max print @ 300 DPI (4x)
13.6 × 13.6 in

Print tip: Always use the "Upscale (Subtle)" option for printing — it preserves the original image character while doubling resolution. "Upscale (Creative)" adds new detail that may change the image significantly. For large format prints, run the 2x upscale first, then apply an external AI upscaler like Topaz Gigapixel to reach 8192 pixels or beyond.

DALL-E 3 via ChatGPT or OpenAI API

DALL-E 3 is the most accessible AI image tool for most people, but it has the most limited resolution options of the major generators.

Print tip: DALL-E 3 images almost always require external AI upscaling for any print larger than a greeting card. Run the downloaded image through Topaz Gigapixel or Magnific AI before sending to a print lab. The landscape mode (1792 × 1024) gives you a better starting point for wide prints but the height is still limiting.

Stable Diffusion SDXL and SD 1.5

Stable Diffusion offers the most flexibility of any AI image tool because it's open source — but that also means its default resolution depends on which version and which interface you're using.

SD 1.5 default
512 × 512 px
SDXL default
1024 × 1024 px
SDXL with Hi-Res Fix
Up to 2048 × 2048 px
With Ultimate SD Upscaler
4096 px and beyond

Print tip: If you're using SDXL, always enable Hi-Res Fix in your UI (Automatic1111, ComfyUI) before generating. This does a two-pass generation that significantly improves sharpness and reaches a higher native resolution. The Ultimate SD Upscaler extension can then push images to print-ready sizes. Avoid SD 1.5 for any print work — 512px is simply too small to work with.

Adobe Firefly Firefly Image 3

Adobe Firefly is designed with creative professionals in mind, and its output resolution reflects that — it's currently one of the highest-resolution outputs available from a consumer AI image tool without additional upscaling.

Default output
2048 × 2048 px
Widescreen output
2688 × 1536 px
Max print @ 300 DPI
6.8 × 6.8 in (square)
Max print @ 150 DPI
13.6 × 13.6 in (square)

Print tip: Firefly's 2048px default is a genuine advantage for printing. At 150 DPI — perfectly acceptable for wall art viewed from a normal distance — you can print up to 13.6 inches without any upscaling. For larger prints, Firefly images upscale exceptionally well because of their clean native detail.

Flux Flux.1 Dev and Pro — Black Forest Labs

Flux is a newer entrant but has rapidly become one of the highest-quality open-source AI image models, with strong detail retention that makes it particularly well-suited to photography-style output.

Default output
1024 × 1024 px
Dev model max
2048 × 2048 px
Max print @ 300 DPI (dev)
6.8 × 6.8 in
Max print @ 150 DPI (dev)
13.6 × 13.6 in

Print tip: Flux's photorealistic output style upscales particularly well because it generates clean edges and fine detail rather than the painterly softness of some other models. If you're creating AI imagery intended for printing, Flux via the dev model at 2048px is an excellent choice before applying external upscaling.

The Upscaling Chain: Getting AI Images to Large Format

For prints larger than 12 inches, every AI image generator — including Midjourney — requires some form of additional upscaling. The most reliable approach is a two-step chain:

  1. Step 1 — Use your AI tool's maximum upscale. Midjourney 4x, Firefly's highest quality export, SDXL with Hi-Res Fix at 2048px. This gives you the best possible starting file.
  2. Step 2 — Apply external AI upscaling. Take that file into Topaz Gigapixel AI or Magnific AI and upscale 2–4x. Topaz works particularly well for photographic-style AI images. Magnific adds detail creatively, which suits painterly or illustrated AI output better.

A 4096 × 4096 pixel Midjourney image put through Topaz at 2x produces an 8192 × 8192 pixel file — enough for a 27 × 27 inch print at 300 DPI. That's genuine large-format capability from an AI source.

For a complete guide to the upscaling options and how to apply them, see: AI Upscaling: Print Small Files Large.

Checking Your AI Image Before You Print

Before sending any AI-generated image to a print lab, run it through a resolution check. The biggest cause of wasted print orders is discovering at the lab (or when the print arrives) that the image wasn't large enough for the chosen size.

The process is simple:

  1. Export your final AI image (after upscaling if needed) as a PNG or TIFF file.
  2. Upload it to PrintSizeChecker.com — the tool reads your pixel dimensions and tells you the maximum print size at each quality level.
  3. If the quality rating is GOOD or EXCELLENT at your target size, you're ready to order.
  4. If it's FAIR or POOR, upscale further before ordering.

This is particularly important for AI images because the quality label displayed in your AI tool's interface is about the image's visual quality, not its print suitability. An "upscaled" image in Midjourney might look beautiful on screen but still be short of the pixel count needed for a large print at 300 DPI.

File Format: What to Use for AI Art Going to Print

The format you save your AI image in matters as much as the resolution:

  • PNG — lossless compression, no quality degradation. Best for most AI art going to print, especially anything with flat colours, sharp edges, or illustration styles.
  • TIFF — uncompressed, the gold standard for professional print labs. Larger file sizes but maximum quality. Use this if your lab accepts it.
  • JPEG — avoid for final print files. JPEG compression introduces artefacts that become visible at large print sizes, especially in smooth gradients (which AI images often feature heavily).
  • WebP — not suitable for print. A web format, not supported by most professional print labs.

Summary: Which AI Tool Is Best for Printing?

If print quality is your primary goal when choosing an AI image tool:

  • Best overall for printing: Midjourney — the 4x upscale gets you further without external tools than any competitor.
  • Best default resolution without upscaling: Adobe Firefly — the 2048px default is genuinely print-ready for medium prints at 150 DPI.
  • Best for photorealistic prints with upscaling: Flux.1 Dev — clean, detailed output that upscales exceptionally well.
  • Most flexible for print workflows: Stable Diffusion XL — with Hi-Res Fix and the Ultimate SD Upscaler, you have full control over output resolution.
  • Least suitable for printing: DALL-E 3 and SD 1.5 — both require external upscaling for virtually any print use case.
